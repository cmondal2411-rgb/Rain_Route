How Rain Route Works

Scientific Methodology, Mathematical Formulation, and Algorithmic Workflow for Class 11 Science Exhibition.

Scientific Research Question:

“Can locally collected rain and waterlogging data improve route selection during rainy conditions?”

Scientific Hypothesis:

“A route-selection system that considers recent waterlogging information can identify a more practical and safer route than one based only on distance.”

Independent Variable:

Rain conditions along route segments, specifically water depth (cm), drainage duration (min), rainfall intensity, and road passability status.

Dependent Variable:

The calculated Rain Risk Score, the Rain-Adjusted Route Cost, and the final selected path recommendation for the traveler.

Controlled Variables:

Physical straight-line road distances, baseline dry-weather vehicular travel speeds, and standard topographical layout of the simulated urban grid.

Limitations & Future Scope:

Crowdsourced report latency, variable driver clearance heights, and sensor calibration. Future extensions could incorporate IoT ultrasonic water level sensors installed directly inside municipal drains.

Mathematical Cost & Risk Formulation

The routing engine shifts from a 1-dimensional metric (distance) to a multi-variable optimization function:

1. Normal Cost = Distance (km) + (Travel Time (min) × 0.25)

2. Rain Risk Score = Depth Factor (40%) + Duration Factor (30%) + Passability Factor (25%) + Recency (5%)

3. Rain-Adjusted Cost = Normal Cost + (Risk Score / 100 × 8.0)
  • • Rain Mode OFF: Navigation minimizes Normal Cost → Selects Route A (2.4 km).
  • • Rain Mode ON: Navigation minimizes Rain-Adjusted Cost → Selects Route B (3.0 km) because its risk penalty is negligible compared to Route A's severe 12 cm flood penalty.
